# Remarks on paper (not checked by Lean)

The terms are those of `README.md`: `d(A, B) = dim A/(A ∩ B)`, `δ(A, B) = d(A, B) + d(B, A)`, families, invariant
subspaces, and finite sums of finite intersections of members. We write *SI subspace* for a finite sum of finite
intersections of members.

Words used for the checks:

- *Paper proof*: one model wrote the proof, and at least one other agent read it line by line.
- *Blind check*: another model received only the statement, without the proof, and had to prove it or refute it.
- *Exact computation*: a program with integer or finite-field arithmetic only, no floating point.

None of these checks is a check by Lean. No professional mathematician has refereed these remarks.

## B1. The one-sided bound needs order `r^2`

**Statement.** Let `q` be a prime power. There are a finite group `G`, a complex vector space `V` and one orbit of subspaces
with `d(A_x, A_y) ≤ r := q` for all members, such that every invariant `W` with `d(W, A_x) ≤ r` for all `x` has
`d(A_x, W) = q^2 = r^2`. So in the one-sided form of Theorem A the second bound must be at least `r^2` for infinitely many
`r`. A linear bound is impossible, and the bound `r (r+1)` of S1 is optimal up to the factor `(r+1)/r`.

**Proof.** Let `P` be the set of the `q^2 + q + 1` points of the projective plane over the field with `q` elements, and
`V = ℂ^P` with basis `e_p`. For each line `ℓ` put `A_ℓ = span{e_p : p ∉ ℓ}`. A line has `q + 1` points, so
`dim A_ℓ = q^2`. Two different lines meet in one point, so `A_ℓ ∩ A_m` has dimension `q^2 − q` and `d(A_ℓ, A_m) = q`.
Let `G` be the group of permutations of `P` that come from `PGL_3(F_q)`, together with all sign changes of coordinates.
It maps the family to itself, and it is transitive on the lines. Let `W` be invariant. The sign change at `p` maps `W` to
itself, so `W` contains the projection of each of its vectors onto `ℂ e_p`. So `W` is spanned by the `e_p` with `p` in some
set `T`, and `T` is invariant under `PGL_3(F_q)`, which is transitive on points. So `W = 0` or `W = V`. But
`d(V, A_ℓ) = q + 1 > r`. So `W = 0`, and `d(A_ℓ, 0) = q^2`.

**Checks.** Paper proof. The example is in the notes of the single-orbit work and in the proof text of GPT-6.2 Astra
(`PROOF.md`, Section 6). A Claude agent read it. An exact computation for `q = 2` (the Fano plane, `r = 2`) gives
`d(A, W) = 4 = r^2` for the subspace that the proof of S2 builds.

## B2. Total error `2r − 1` for one orbit

**Statement.** For each `k ≥ 2` and `r = 2^(k−2)` there is one orbit of a finite group in a complex vector space of
dimension `4r − 1`, with `d(A_x, A_y) = r` for different members, such that every subspace `W` (invariant or not) has
`δ(A_x, W) ≥ 2r − 1` for some member. So statement 1 of the single-orbit case (`δ < 2r` for one orbit of a finite group) is
optimal.

**Proof.** `PROOF.md`, Section 6. The members are the coordinate subspaces on the sets `{v ≠ 0 : λ(v) = 1}` in
`ℂ^(F_2^k − {0})`, one for each nonzero linear form `λ`. The group is generated by `GL_k(F_2)` and the sign changes. The
average of `δ(A_x, S)` over the members has a least minimizer, which is invariant, so it is `0` or `V`. Its minimum
value is `2r − 1`, and the maximum over the members is at least the average.

**Checks.** Paper proof, read by two agents.

## B3. Total error for families: `3r` holds, `2r` fails

**Statement (a).** For every family there is an invariant `W` with `δ(A_x, W) ≤ 3r` for every `x`. Any group, any field,
any dimension.

**Proof.** `PROOF.md`, Section 4. One measure gives a chain of invariant near-minimizers `S_1 ⊇ ... ⊇ S_m` at the levels
`j/(m+1)`. The average of `δ(A_x, S_j)` over `j` is at most about `2r` for every `x`. Choosing one member of the chain for
all `x` costs at most another `r`.

**Checks.** Paper proof by GPT-6.2 Astra, read line by line by an independent Claude agent (verdict: correct). Exact
computation over `F_2` for 50 random invariant families. A blind check by Gemini 4 Neon was started; its verdict is not
included here.

**Statement (b).** The constant 3 cannot be replaced by 2. There are finite families in which every invariant `W` has
`δ(A_x, W) > 2r` for some member.

- Example 1 (`PROOF.md`, Section 5): a finite group, a complex coordinate space of dimension `ML` with `M = 8192^6` and
  `L = 4369`, and `r = M(4L − 1)/16`. Every invariant `W` has `max_x δ(A_x, W) ≥ ML/2 = (2 + 2/17475) r`.
  Checks: paper proof by GPT-6.2 Astra, read line by line by an independent Claude agent; the arithmetic is exact; the
  estimate on translates (inequality (8) of Section 5) was checked by exact computation for `q = 4` and `q = 8`.
- Example 2: the cyclic group `Z/N`, `N = n_1 n_2 n_3 n_4` with the primes `n_1, ..., n_4 = 23789, 16067, 34253, 22877`
  (for each of them, 2 is a primitive root), acting on the disjoint union of the four cycles `Z/n_i`. Three subsets and all
  their translates form the family. Then `r = 20368`, and every invariant subset `W`, and every invariant subspace `W` of
  the `F_2`-vector space on the same points, has `max_x δ(A_x, W) ≥ 41164`, about `2.021 r`. Checks: exact computation with
  integers, by two independent programs; the second one was written before its author read the first one. The data
  (three subsets) are not in this repository.

**Sets.** Example 2 also gives a family of sets in which no invariant set is within `2r` of every member. This agrees
with the results of P. M. Neumann (1996) as its zbMATH review (Zbl 0865.20004) states them. His Theorem 1.1, with the
invariant set within `2m − 1`, is about one set and its translates (one orbit). His Theorem 3.4, about infinite
close-knit families, has no group action, and the set that it gives is not said to be invariant.
The closing section of P. M. Neumann, Arch. Math. 67 (1996) 265-274, was available to us only through its zbMATH review.

## B4. Total error with the form of Theorem A

**Statement (a), lower bound.** For infinitely many `r` there is one orbit of a finite cyclic group, in a complex vector
space, with `d(gA, hA) ≤ r` for all `g, h`, such that every invariant SI subspace `W` has
`δ(A, W) ≥ c r log r / log log r`. Here `c > 0` is an absolute constant. So with the form of Theorem A, no linear bound
for the total error exists, although an invariant `W` with total error less than `2r` exists for one orbit of a finite
group (statement 1 of the single-orbit case).

**Construction.** Choose integers `t ≥ 2`, `m ≥ t + 1`, `b ≥ 1`, and a set `D ⊆ Z/m` such that every subset of `Z/m` with at
most `t` elements has a translate inside `D` (a *t-universal* set). Put `a = |D|` and `α = max_{k ≠ 0} |D \ (D + k)|`.
Let `ω` be a primitive `m`-th root of unity. The group `G = Z/m` acts on `Q = ℂ^(Z/m)` (basis `e_k`) by translation, and on
`U = ℂ^(t+1)` by `k · (u_0, ..., u_t) = (ω^{jk} u_j)_j`. Put `V = U^b ⊕ Q`, `K = (ker σ)^b ⊆ U^b` where
`σ(u) = u_0 + ... + u_t`, and `A = K ⊕ span{e_d : d ∈ D}`.

- *Diameter.* `d(A, gA) = d(K, gK) + |D \ (D + g)| ≤ b + α =: r` (here `\` is the difference of sets).
- *Intersections.* For a finite set `S ⊆ G`, `⋂_{g ∈ S} gA = K_S ⊕ span{e_y : y ∈ D_S}` with `K_S = ⋂ gK` and
  `D_S = ⋂ (g + D)`. If `|S| ≤ t`, then `D_S` is not empty (a translate of `−S` lies in `D`). If `|S| ≥ t + 1`, then
  `K_S = 0` (the functionals that define the `gK` are Vandermonde vectors, and any `t + 1` of them are independent).
- *Bound.* Let `W` be an invariant SI subspace. If some summand has `|S| ≤ t`, then `W` contains some `e_y`, hence all of
  `Q`, so `W = Y ⊕ Q` with `Y = W ∩ U^b`, and `δ(A, W) = (m − a) + δ(K, Y)`. The characters `k ↦ ω^{jk}`
  (`j = 0, ..., t`) are distinct, so the invariant subspace `Y` is `⊕_j f_j ⊗ Y_j` with `Y_j ⊆ ℂ^b` (`f_j` the basis of
  `U`). The map `Y → ℂ^b`, `(y_j) ↦ ∑_j y_j`, has kernel `Y ∩ K` and an image `I` of some dimension `s ≤ b`, and each
  `Y_j` lies in `I`. So `d(Y, K) = s`, `d(K, Y) = tb − dim Y + s ≥ tb − ts`, and `δ(K, Y) ≥ tb − (t − 1)s ≥ b`. This
  gives `δ(A, W) ≥ (m − a) + b`. Otherwise every summand lies in `Q` and
  contains some `e_y` unless it is `0`, so `W = 0` or `W = Q`. Here `δ(A, 0) = tb + a` and
  `δ(A, Q) = tb + m − a ≥ m − a + b`. So in all cases `δ(A, W) ≥ min(m − a + b, tb + a)`.
- *Asymptotics.* A random choice gives a t-universal set with `a ≤ 2 m^{1 − 1/t} (t^3 log m)^{1/t}`. With `t` about
  `log m / (3 log log m)` and `b` about `m / t`, the ratio `min(m − a + b, tb + a) / r` grows like `t`, which is of order
  `log r / log log r`.
- *An explicit case.* `m = 500`, `t = 3`, `a = 118`, `α = 99`, `b = 132`: `r = 231`, and every invariant SI subspace has
  `δ(A, W) ≥ 514 > 2r = 462`. The invariant subspace `U^b` (not an SI subspace) has `δ(A, U^b) = a + b = 250`.

**Checks.** Paper proof by a Claude agent. Two blind checks, by Gemini 4 Neon and by GPT-6.2 Astra, both with
independent proofs: true. Exact computation of all SI subspaces for `m = 13` (field `F_53`, `t = 2`, `b = 2`,
`D = {0, 1, 3, 9}`): the minimum is 8, as predicted; the same check was repeated by a second agent. Exact checks also for
`m = 15` and `m = 21`.

**Statement (b), upper bound.** For every family there is an invariant SI subspace `D` with
`δ(D, A_x) ≤ 2r − 1 + r (1 + ⌊log₂ 2r⌋)` for every `x`. This is `O(r log r)`.

**Proof.** `PROOF.md`, Section 3, formula (4): the descent of S2 at a level just above `1/2`.

**Checks.** Paper proof by GPT-6.2 Astra, read line by line by an independent Claude agent (verdict: correct).

**Statement (c), one orbit of a finite group.** For one orbit of a finite group the best bound for the total error with an
invariant SI subspace is of order `r log r / log log r`.

- Lower bound: (a).
- Upper bound: for one orbit of a finite group, over any field, and for every `θ` with `0 < θ < 1`, there is an invariant
  SI subspace `W` with `d(W, A) ≤ r/θ` and `d(A, W) ≤ r ⌈log(1 + r/(1 − θ)) / log(1/θ)⌉`. With `θ = 1/2` this gives
  `δ(A, W) ≤ r (2 + ⌈log₂(2r + 1)⌉)`, and a choice of `θ` that tends to `0` slowly gives
  `δ(A, W) ≤ (1 + o(1)) r log r / log log r`. Proof idea: take the largest maximizer `M` of
  `U ↦ E_B dim(U ∩ B) − θ dim U` over all subspaces `U` (average over the members `B`); `M` is invariant. Starting from
  `J = A`, intersect `J` with members, each time with a member that reduces the dimension of the image of `J` in `V/M` to at
  most `θ` times its value, until `J ⊆ M`. Then `W = ∑_g gJ`.
- Checks of the upper bound: paper proof by GPT-6.2 Astra, read by a Claude agent; blind check by Gemini 4 Neon: true.
- The constant 1 in a matching lower asymptotic `(1 − o(1)) r log r / log log r` is checked only by its author, a
  Claude agent. We do not claim it.

## B5. The minimizer of the proof need not have the form of Theorem A

**Statement.** Even a unique minimizer of the weighted function used in the proofs can fail to be an SI subspace.

- *Three members in `k^5`* (`PROOF.md`, Section 3, last part): `V = U ⊕ k^3` with `dim U = 2`, three distinct lines
  `L_i` in `U`, and `A_i = L_i ⊕ k e_i`. For equal weights and a level `t` with `1/3 < t < 1/2`, the unique minimizer is `U`.
  All pairwise intersections of the `A_i` are `0`, and no `A_i` lies in `U`. So `U` is not an SI subspace. Any field.
  Checks: paper proof, read by an independent Claude agent; exact computation over `F_2`.
- *One orbit of the Klein four group in `k^7`*, over every field: the least minimizer of `∑_g δ(gA, S)` has dimension 3,
  and the intersections of members inside it span only a subspace of dimension 2. Checks: paper proof by a Claude agent;
  exact computation over `F_2`, `F_3` and `F_5`, and again over `F_2` by a second agent.

This is the reason why the proof of S2 passes from the near-minimizer `W` to the smaller subspace `D`, the sum of all
finite intersections of members inside `W`.
